Hi ho, Kermit the Fraud here

Medium
Wood, twine, acrylic teeth, and a Kermit the Frog plush
Size
6′ × 5.5′ × 4′
Year
2026

In 1955, Jim Henson created the first Kermit the Frog at UMD out of his mother’s old coat and a halved ping-pong ball. Seventy years later, Disney owns him, and on this campus he has become a statue, a photo op, and a cheerful brand ambassador. Beyond the university, he appears on gas-station bootlegs, overseas knockoffs, and the darkest corners of the internet.

Once you put art into the world, you lose control of it; people can sell it, exploit it, and twist it into something unrecognizable while your name stays attached. I wanted to highlight that with this sculpture. Impaled through his mouth and with his stomach cut open, this mass-produced Kermit slowly sheds his stuffing onto the floor. And unlike Henson’s original puppet—which had no stuffing, only the human hand within—this version gradually empties itself before the viewer.
